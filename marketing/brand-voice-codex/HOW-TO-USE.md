## What This Skill Does

Converts a brand's voice from founder instinct into transmittable
doctrine. Voice separated from tone (character versus weather), five
written definitions with this-not-that fences, canonical examples and
anti-examples doing the teaching, a tone map that earns its keep in
the hard contexts, explicit sub-brand inheritance, and a drift watch
so the voice evolves by decision instead of eroding one post at a
time.

## When It Activates

- Creating or overhauling a brand voice guide
- Keeping copy consistent across channels, writers, and AI tools
- Onboarding collaborators to write in-voice
- "This doesn't sound like us"
- Managing sub-brands under one parent identity

## Installation

1. Create a folder named `brand-voice-codex` in your Claude skills
   location.
2. Place `SKILL.md` inside it.
3. Claude activates it automatically on voice and brand-language work.
4. Pairs with `conversion-copy-doctrine` (what the copy says; this
   codex governs how it sounds) and `organic-content-engine` (the
   cadence the voice publishes on).

## Example Invocations

> "Build a voice guide for my brand."

The five definitions, extracted from your best existing work:
character traits defined and fenced, stance chosen, lexicon with the
banned list, signature moves named — anchored by annotated canonical
passages and the anti-examples that teach the near-miss.

> "My brand has multiple sub-brands with different audiences."

Sub-brand architecture: each states what it inherits from the parent
and what it expresses in its own register, with both-direction
enforcement — so a playful children's-brand register and a
precision-operations register can share DNA without contaminating
each other.

> "Every writer we hire takes months to sound like us."

The onboarding package: codex plus rewrite demonstrations — the same
message off-voice and on-voice, side by side — because the
transformation is the training, and it compresses months into a
reading.

> "Write our apology for the shipping failure."

The tone map's hard-context row: register steady, ownership plain,
character held — an apology that is still recognizably the brand,
not a lawyer wearing its logo.

## What You Get

- A voice that survives delegation, scale, and AI tooling
- Traits fenced so "bold" cannot drift into obnoxious
- Hard-context tone guidance where voice guides usually go silent
- Sub-brands that share DNA without register contamination
- Drift caught at its source, on a cadence
- A voice that changes on the record, never by accident

## What This Skill Will Refuse

- Voice guidance that lives only as instinct
- Trait words without fences or examples
- One register forced across every context
- Implicit sub-brand inheritance
- Voice evolution by unreviewed accumulation
