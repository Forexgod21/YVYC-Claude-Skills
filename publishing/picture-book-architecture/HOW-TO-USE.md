## What This Skill Does

Holds picture book and read-aloud manuscripts to the craft
architecture of the form: composition by spread and page turn, the
32-page frame as a beat budget, a strict labor split between words
and art, the read-aloud test on every draft, word-count discipline,
child-owned emotional arcs, and design for the four-hundredth
reading.

## The Problem It Solves

Writers treat picture books as short stories with pictures. The
results are predictable: text that describes its own illustrations,
spreads with no reason to turn, rhyme with bent meter, morals
delivered as sermons, adults rescuing child protagonists, and
manuscripts that were never once read aloud before submission.
Editors reject these in minutes; the few that slip through die in the
living room, where a parent has to perform them nightly.

## When It Activates

- Writing, revising, critiquing, or structuring a picture book, board
  book, early reader, or any children's text meant to be read aloud
- Planning spread maps, page breaks, or illustration notes
- Evaluating children's manuscripts for submission, acquisition, or
  series consistency
- Any children's book task where the word count is low enough to look
  deceptively small

## Installation

1. Create a folder named `picture-book-architecture` in your Claude
   skills location.
2. Place `SKILL.md` inside it.
3. Pair it with `narrative-spine-architecture` (story structure
   underneath) and `prose-precision-protocol` (line editing inside
   the word-count law).

## Example Invocations

> "Write a picture book about a girl who's afraid of the dark."

The spread map comes first: beats mapped to roughly 14 spreads, each
turn loaded, the girl owning both the fear and its resolution. Then
the text, carrying only what art cannot: sound, interiority, time,
voice.

> "Here's my manuscript, what do you think?"

The architecture critique: which spreads stall, which lines the art
will carry, where the read-aloud stumbles, whether the meter survives
a stranger's mouth, and who actually solves the problem on the final
spread.

> "Should this rhyme?"

The promise stated plainly: rhyme is a commitment to flawless meter
under a natural reading. If the draft cannot keep that promise on
every line, the recommendation is prose, with the reasoning shown.

> "Add illustration notes."

Only where the text depends on the art contradicting or completing
it. Everything else is the illustrator's authority, and a note-heavy
manuscript gets flagged as the trust failure editors read it as.

## What Changes In Claude's Behavior

| Without This Skill | With This Skill |
|---|---|
| Prose with pictures assumed | Composition by spread and turn, mapped before polish |
| Text describing what the art will show | The labor split enforced; the art's share cut from the text |
| Word count treated as difficulty level | The shorter the text, the harsher the standard per word |
| Rhyme kept because it took effort | Flawless meter or prose, no third option |
| Morals announced by characters | Meaning carried by events, deduced by the child |
| Adults resolving the child's arc | The protagonist earns the ending |
| Drafts judged silently on the page | Every draft performed aloud, stumbles hunted |

## What This Skill Will Refuse

- Shipping a manuscript that has not been read aloud
- Keeping rhyme with bent meter because the rhyme is precious
- Letting an adult character rescue the protagonist
- Ending on a stated moral
- Illustration notes that direct the illustrator's own authority
- Sanding out every interesting word in the name of age level
- Treating the 32-page frame as negotiable before the story problem
  is solved
