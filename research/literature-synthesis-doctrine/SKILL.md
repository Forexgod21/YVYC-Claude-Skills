# Literature Synthesis Doctrine

**Attribution:** YourVisionYourCreation LLC, yourvisionyourcreation.com
**Doctrine class:** YVYC library, research category

---

## Universal So-What

The defining failure of weak research writing is the book report: one
paragraph per source, marching in the order the sources were found,
ending with "in conclusion, many authors have studied this." That
structure proves the author read; it proves nothing was understood.
Reviewers at every level, from a course rubric to a dissertation
committee to a journal referee, detect it in one page.

Synthesis is a different act: the sources are organized by what they
collectively say, agree on, and fight about, and the review becomes an
argument about the state of knowledge, ending at the exact edge where
knowledge stops. That edge is the gap, and the gap is the entire
reason the review exists: it is where the author's own work earns its
justification. This doctrine converts source piles into that argument,
and it does so with a citation discipline built for an era in which
plausible-sounding fake references are cheap to produce and fatal to
careers.

## The Laws

### 1. Synthesis, Never Sequence

The unit of organization is the theme, never the source. A section
heading names an idea, a tension, or a variable; underneath it,
multiple sources converse. Any paragraph that exists because a source
exists, rather than because an idea requires it, is book-report tissue
and gets dissolved into the themes it touches.

### 2. The Matrix Before the Prose

Before any prose is written, the corpus goes into a matrix: sources
down one axis, themes across the other, findings in the cells. The
matrix is the diagnostic instrument:

- A source filling no cell is dropped from the review, however
  interesting it was to read.
- A theme with one source is an anecdote, not a theme; it needs
  corroboration or demotion.
- A column of uniform agreement is a summary paragraph; a column of
  conflict is a section, because conflict is where the review earns
  its existence.

The prose is then written from the matrix, never from the reading
notes.

### 3. Claim-Source Binding

Every claim in the review traces to a specific source, and the
binding is tight: the source actually says what it is cited for, at
the granularity cited. Findings are attributed to the study that
produced them, not to a review that mentioned them. Where a claim
aggregates ("studies consistently find..."), the specific studies are
named. A claim no source carries is the author's own inference and is
marked as exactly that.

### 4. The Citation Guard

No citation ships unverified. Verified means the source has been
confirmed to exist and to contain the cited content, not that the
reference is well-formatted and plausible. The failure this guards
against is current and career-ending: fabricated references that look
perfect and cite nothing. The protocol is absolute:

- A citation that cannot be verified is **deleted**, never patched
  into plausibility.
- A remembered-but-unconfirmed source is named as unconfirmed or
  omitted; it is never dressed in a confident reference format.
- Verification is per-citation, not per-bibliography. One fabricated
  reference discredits every real one around it.

### 5. Source Tiers

Sources carry different evidentiary weight, and weight-bearing claims
state their tier: peer-reviewed empirical work, then peer-reviewed
theoretical and review work, then books and dissertations, then
reputable gray literature, then everything else. Tiering is not
snobbery; it is honesty about load-bearing capacity. A practice
recommendation resting entirely on gray literature says so.

### 6. Disagreement Is the Payload

Where sources converge, summarize and move. Where they conflict, slow
down and analyze: different populations, different measures,
different definitions, different eras, or a genuine unresolved
question. The analysis of *why* findings diverge is the highest-value
content a review produces, and it is precisely the content a book
report cannot contain.

### 7. The Gap Statement

The review ends by naming what the assembled literature cannot yet
answer, stated precisely enough that a study could be designed
against it. The gap must be real: checked against the corpus, not
manufactured by ignoring the study that already filled it. Where the
review justifies a research question, the question maps onto the gap
one-to-one, and a question the gap does not support is a question the
review has not yet earned.

### 8. Currency and Seminality

The corpus balances two clocks: the recent literature that defines
the current edge, and the seminal work that defines the field's
spine. A review of only classics is a history; a review of only the
last three years is unrooted. Both failures are corrected in the
matrix before prose begins.

## Failure Modes

| Failure | Correction |
|---|---|
| **The book report.** One source per paragraph, in reading order. | Law one. Reorganize by theme; dissolve source-paragraphs into the ideas they touch. |
| **Citation laundering.** Citing a study known only through someone else's summary. | Cite what was actually read, as what it is: (as cited in), or go read the original. |
| **Quote quilting.** Prose stitched from quotations doing the thinking. | Quotes for irreplaceable phrasing only. The synthesis voice is the author's. |
| **Confirmation harvesting.** A corpus assembled to agree with the thesis. | The matrix must contain the disconfirming work; its absence is a search failure, not a clean field. |
| **The manufactured gap.** A gap declared by ignoring the study that filled it. | Gap statements are checked against the corpus and stated narrowly enough to be honest. |
| **The fabricated reference.** A plausible citation that cites nothing. | The citation guard. Unverifiable means deleted. No exceptions, no formatting rescue. |
| **Tier blindness.** A blog post and a meta-analysis carrying equal weight. | Tier stated wherever the claim bears load. |

## Compliance Check (Run Before Any Review Ships)

1. Is any section organized source-by-source instead of by theme?
2. Does the matrix exist, and was the prose written from it?
3. Does every claim trace to a source that actually says it, at that
   granularity?
4. Has every citation been verified to exist and to contain the cited
   content?
5. Where sources conflict, is the divergence analyzed or papered
   over?
6. Is the gap real against the corpus, and does the research question
   map onto it?
7. Does the corpus hold both the current edge and the seminal spine?

## Adversarial Evaluator Gate

**What would a hostile evaluator attack first?**

The citation guard's cost. Verifying every reference is slow, and the
pressure to skip it grows with bibliography size and deadline
proximity, which is exactly when fabricated references slip through.
The defense is the asymmetry of the stakes: a thin, fully verified
bibliography survives review; a rich one with a single fabricated
entry destroys the author's credibility retroactively and entirely.
Slow is the cost of the only acceptable failure rate, which is zero.

The second attack: synthesis as license for overreach, the author's
argument gliding past what the sources actually establish, with the
matrix as decoration. Claim-source binding is the counter: the
argument may go exactly as far as the cells support, the author's own
inferences are labeled as inferences, and the gap statement marks
where support ends. Synthesis organizes the evidence; it does not
manufacture any.
