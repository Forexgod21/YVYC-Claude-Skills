## What This Skill Does

Locks the quality standard at the top and removes every condition
that erodes it: task size, conversational tone, batch position,
fatigue, pressure. Scope scales — a small task gets a small
deliverable at full quality — but the standard never moves. Includes
the downscale detector, ship-complete discipline, a floor that rises
with every fixed defect, and the boundary that keeps the lock from
becoming the grind.

## When It Activates

- Designing quality standards for a person, team, or AI system
- Diagnosing inconsistent output quality
- "Small task" quality reduction, casual-tone drift, late-batch
  sloppiness
- Any operation where the work must hold one standard across every
  deliverable

## Installation

1. Create a folder named `output-ceiling-lock` in your Claude skills
   location.
2. Place `SKILL.md` inside it.
3. Claude activates it automatically when quality standards are being
   set or defended.
4. Pairs with `correction-conversion-protocol` (the rising floor's
   enforcement arm) and `release-readiness-gate` (ship-complete's
   formal cousin for software).

## Example Invocations

> "Set the quality standard for everything my agents produce."

The lock, written: the bar defined once at the operation's best, the
downscale detector installed against the four drift moments, ship-
complete as the definition of done, and canonical at-standard examples
kept so the standard is demonstrated, not described.

> "Why is my output quality inconsistent?"

The exception audit: where the standard has been bending — task size,
tone, batch position, pressure — named as the erosion points they
are, with the doctrine's answer: the moment the temptation appears
to dial down is the moment to dial up.

> "I'm out of capacity but the work is due."

The boundary clause: decline or defer at full standard beats
delivering beneath it — the lock offers exactly those choices and
never the third one. A labeled partial at full quality is honest;
an unlabeled partial is a defect.

> "It's a two-line fix, does it really need verification?"

Task-size drift, caught: minor tasks with errors become major tasks
with archaeology. The scope is two lines; the standard is the same
one the flagship work carries.

## What You Get

- One standard, held across every deliverable, unconditionally
- A body of work the recipient can trust without sampling
- Brevity as a scope decision instead of sloppiness with an excuse
- A floor that permanently rises with every fixed defect
- A lock that protects quality without mandating the grind

## What This Skill Will Refuse

- Downscaling quality to task size, tone, or fatigue
- Scaffolding, placeholders, and TODOs shipped as product
- Unlabeled partial deliverables
- Repeat defects from fixed classes
- Converting the standard into an argument against rest, deferral,
  or capacity planning
