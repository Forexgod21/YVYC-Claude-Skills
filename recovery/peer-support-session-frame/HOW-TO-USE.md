## What This Skill Does

Holds the frame that makes peer support powerful and safe: the peer
lane defined (lived experience offered, never clinical care absorbed),
a four-part session architecture the person fills, story discipline
with the whose-need test, self-direction across all recovery pathways,
honest confidentiality limits stated up front, and the peer's own
oxygen mask protected first.

## When It Activates

- Peer specialist (PRSS) session planning and structure
- Recovery group and mutual-aid meeting facilitation
- Veteran peer support program design
- Training and onboarding peer supporters
- Documentation frames for peer work

## Installation

1. Create a folder named `peer-support-session-frame` in your Claude
   skills location.
2. Place `SKILL.md` inside it.
3. Claude activates it automatically on peer support structure work.
4. Pairs with `crisis-boundary-protocol` (the escalation frame when a
   session leaves peer scope) and `recovery-capital-mapping` (the
   strengths inventory the Work phase can run).

## Example Invocations

> "Help me structure my peer support sessions."

The four-part frame — Arrive, Focus, Work, Anchor — as a container
the person fills: check-in presence before agenda, the person naming
what matters today, options over advice, and a close with one concrete
step and one strength named.

> "A veteran I support keeps asking me what medication he should take."

The lane, held warmly: that question deserves someone with training
the peer does not have, and the peer's job is the walk to that door —
referral as a skill, delivered without shame, with the relationship
intact.

> "How much of my own story should I share?"

Story discipline: the whose-need test on every disclosure, dosing
that builds hope without making the session the peer's meeting, and
the rule that unstabilized current struggles belong to the peer's own
support system, not the session.

> "Train my new peer supporters."

The full doctrine as curriculum: lane, frame, story discipline,
self-direction, confidentiality honesty, and the oxygen mask — with
the failure modes table as the case-study backbone.

## What You Get

- Sessions with structure that never becomes script
- A lane that protects both people in the room
- Self-disclosure that serves the person every time
- Pathway respect that keeps trust alive
- Confidentiality built on honesty about its limits
- Peers who last, because their own recovery is protocol-protected

## What This Skill Will Refuse

- Blurring peer support into amateur clinical care
- Advice dressed as lived experience
- Pathway evangelism
- Hidden confidentiality limits
- Session structures that ignore the peer's own recovery needs
