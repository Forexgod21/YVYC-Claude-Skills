## What This Skill Does

Builds the bridge before the fire. For any program that puts peer
supporters, volunteers, or mentors in contact with people who may
experience crisis, this skill designs the scope line in calm: written
role boundaries, recognition-without-diagnosis training, the warm
handoff standard, the no-secret-promises honesty doctrine, supporter
debriefs, and the organizational backstop that makes all of it real.

## When It Activates

- Designing crisis escalation procedures for peer or community
  programs
- Scope-of-role training for non-clinical supporters
- Warm handoff and referral pathway design
- Safety protocol development and review
- "What do I do when someone I support is in crisis" — asked at
  program design time, where it belongs

## Installation

1. Create a folder named `crisis-boundary-protocol` in your Claude
   skills location.
2. Place `SKILL.md` inside it.
3. Claude activates it automatically on crisis boundary and
   escalation design work.
4. Completes the recovery fire team: `peer-support-session-frame`
   holds the session, `recovery-capital-mapping` builds the plan,
   this protocol guards the moment that exceeds both.

## Example Invocations

> "Build a crisis protocol for our veteran peer support program."

The full architecture: clinically-informed scope line in writing,
behavioral recognition triggers, when-unsure-escalate as the honored
default, warm handoff standard with a verified directory (988 and
the Veterans Crisis Line option pre-loaded), same-day documentation,
and the supporter debrief structure.

> "Train my peer supporters on boundaries."

Scenario-based training built from the doctrine: stating the scope
line from memory, practicing the warm handoff script, rehearsing the
honesty doctrine ("most of what we talk about stays between us — the
exception is your safety"), and drilling before contact, because a
protocol never rehearsed is a rumor.

> "One of my peers is burning out after a rough escalation."

The debrief frame, with the two questions kept separate: process
review (did the protocol work) and human review (how is the
supporter) — because merging them teaches supporters to hide
escalations, and hidden escalations are how programs fail quietly.

## What You Get

- A scope line drawn in calm, not improvised in fire
- Supporters who recognize without diagnosing and escalate without
  shame
- Handoffs that keep trust: present, informed, followed through
- Confidentiality built on honesty about its limits
- A directory that works when it is needed
- A protocol that improves after every activation

## What This Skill Will Refuse

- Programs that put supporters in contact before scenario training
- Secret-keeping promises around safety
- Escalations done TO people instead of WITH them
- Crisis binders with unverified numbers
- Peer programs with no qualified backstop
