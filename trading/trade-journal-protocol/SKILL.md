# Trade Journal Protocol

**Attribution:** YourVisionYourCreation LLC — yourvisionyourcreation.com
**Doctrine class:** YVYC original — trading category

---

## Universal So-What

Memory is the worst trading analyst ever employed: it remembers the
wins vividly, files the losses under bad luck, and rewrites every
decision to look smarter than it was. The journal exists because the
trader's account is an experiment, and an experiment without records
is a superstition with a balance. What gets recorded can improve;
what gets remembered only flatters.

---

## Core Doctrine

### 1. The Entry Record — Written BEFORE the Trade

The journal's power is the timestamp. Recorded at entry, not after:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Setup name | Which system/pattern from the playbook this is |
| Thesis | Why this trade, one or two sentences |
| Timeframe + context | Trading timeframe, higher-timeframe state |
| Entry, stop, target | Exact levels, with the structural reason for each |
| Risk | Percent of account and dollar amount, per the risk doctrine |
| Planned R:R | The asymmetry at entry |
| State check | One honest line: calm, tired, tilted, rushed, revenge-adjacent |

A journal written after the exit is a memoir, not an instrument —
post-hoc entries inherit every bias the journal exists to catch.

### 2. The Exit Record — Outcome AND Execution, Scored Separately

The core analytical move of the protocol:

- **Outcome:** win, loss, breakeven — in R (risk multiples), not
  dollars, so trades are comparable across sizes
- **Execution grade:** did the trade FOLLOW THE PLAN? Entry as
  planned, stop honored, target managed per rules?
- These two axes are independent, and the four quadrants mean
  different things:

| | Followed plan | Broke plan |
|---|---|---|
| **Won** | System evidence ✓ | POISON — a paid lesson in bad behavior |
| **Lost** | Tuition — normal cost of edge | Compounded failure — review immediately |

The plan-breaking win is the most dangerous cell in trading: it
pays the trader to destroy their own system. The journal flags it
louder than any loss.

### 3. The Deviation Log

Every difference between plan and execution gets one line: what
deviated, and what the trader was feeling when it happened. Over
fifty trades, the deviation log is a map of exactly where discipline
leaks — the specific hour, setup, emotion, or market condition where
the plan loses to the impulse. No lecture fixes what the log has not
first located.

### 4. Review Cadence — Three Altitudes

- **Post-trade (minutes):** record the exit fields while the trade
  is fresh; no analysis, no self-flagellation — data capture only
- **Weekly (30-60 min):** the week's trades against the playbook;
  deviation patterns; one process goal for next week — ONE, not five
- **Monthly/quarterly (deep):** per-setup statistics — win rate,
  average R, expectancy per setup; kill, keep, or scale decisions;
  the state-check field correlated against execution grades

### 5. Sample Size Sobriety

- No setup is judged — kept or killed — on fewer than a defensible
  sample of ITS OWN trades; a handful of outcomes is noise wearing
  a trend costume
- Expectancy is the verdict metric: (win rate × average win) −
  (loss rate × average loss), per setup, in R
- Variance literacy: a system with real edge produces losing streaks
  as a mathematical certainty. The journal answers the only question
  that matters in one: is this streak within the system's recorded
  variance, or outside it? Feel cannot answer that. Records can.

### 6. The Playbook Feedback Loop

The journal is not a diary — it is the input to a living playbook:

- Each setup in the playbook carries its journal-derived stats,
  updated on the review cadence
- Setups below expectancy over a defensible sample get demoted to
  observation-only or killed — in writing, with the stats attached
- New setup candidates trade at minimum size until their own sample
  earns them a full risk budget
- The playbook version-controls: what changed, when, and what
  evidence drove it. A playbook that never changes is not
  discipline — it is a journal nobody read.

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure | Cause | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Journal flatters, teaches nothing | Entries written after exit | Entry record before the trade, timestamped |
| Bad habits getting stronger | Plan-breaking wins celebrated | The poison quadrant, flagged louder than losses |
| Same mistake, month after month | Deviations felt but never located | Deviation log — one line, every difference |
| Good system abandoned in a normal streak | Variance illiterate | Streak checked against recorded variance |
| Broken setup traded for a year | No per-setup expectancy | Monthly per-setup stats; kill/keep/scale in writing |
| Journal kept, never used | Diary mode | Playbook feedback loop with version control |

---

## Non-Negotiables

1. The entry record is written before the trade.
2. Outcome and execution are scored separately — every trade.
3. The plan-breaking win is flagged as poison, always.
4. Every deviation gets its line in the log.
5. No kill/keep verdict without a defensible per-setup sample.
6. The journal feeds the playbook, and the playbook shows its
   receipts.

---

## Boundary Note

This skill governs record-keeping and review discipline. It does not
provide financial advice or evaluate specific instruments. The
statistics it produces describe the trader's own recorded history —
past performance, including the trader's own, guarantees nothing
forward.
