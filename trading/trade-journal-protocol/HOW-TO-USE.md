## What This Skill Does

Replaces memory — the worst trading analyst ever employed — with an
instrument. Entry records written before the trade, outcome and
execution scored as separate axes, the plan-breaking win flagged as
poison, a deviation log that maps exactly where discipline leaks,
and per-setup expectancy stats that feed a version-controlled
playbook.

## When It Activates

- Building or reviewing a trade journal
- Post-trade, weekly, and monthly performance reviews
- "Why am I losing" / "does this strategy have edge"
- Backtest and trade-history analysis
- Turning trading records into system decisions

## Installation

1. Create a folder named `trade-journal-protocol` in your Claude
   skills location.
2. Place `SKILL.md` inside it.
3. Claude activates it automatically on trading performance work.
4. Completes the trading fire team: `chart-pattern-discipline` reads,
   `risk-first-position-doctrine` sizes, this skill keeps the score
   that makes both improvable.

## Example Invocations

> "Set up a trade journal for me."

The full instrument: pre-trade entry record with the state check,
exit record scoring outcome and execution separately, deviation log,
three-altitude review cadence, and the per-setup stats architecture
that will eventually render kill/keep/scale verdicts.

> "I broke my rules but the trade won big."

The poison quadrant, flagged louder than any loss: that trade paid
you to destroy your own system, and the journal records it as the
most expensive win of the month.

> "I've lost six in a row — is my system broken?"

The only honest answer comes from records: the streak checked against
the system's own recorded variance and per-setup expectancy. Within
variance, the process goal is to keep executing. Outside it, the
setup goes to observation-only — in writing, with the stats attached.

> "Review my month."

Per-setup win rate, average R, and expectancy; deviation patterns
correlated against the state-check field; one process goal for the
next period — one, not five.

## What You Get

- A journal that cannot flatter, because it was written before the
  outcome existed
- The win/loss × followed/broke matrix that finds the poison
- A deviation map showing where discipline actually leaks
- Variance literacy that saves good systems from bad streaks
- A playbook that shows its receipts

## What This Skill Will Refuse

- Post-hoc journal entries dressed as records
- Celebrating plan-breaking wins
- Kill/keep verdicts on a handful of trades
- "Feel" as an analyst in drawdown reviews
- A journal that never changes the playbook
