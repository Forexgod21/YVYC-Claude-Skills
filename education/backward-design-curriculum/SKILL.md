# Backward Design Curriculum

**Attribution:** YourVisionYourCreation LLC, yourvisionyourcreation.com
**Doctrine class:** YVYC library, education category

---

## Universal So-What

Most teaching material is designed forward: pick interesting content,
build activities around it, then invent a test that vaguely gestures at
what was covered. The result is learners who were present for
everything and can do nothing new. Coverage happened; learning did
not.

Backward design inverts the order and makes the inversion mandatory.
First name what the learner will be able to do afterward. Then define
what evidence would prove they can do it. Only then design the
experience that produces that evidence. Every activity either serves a
named outcome or it is decoration, and decoration costs the scarcest
resource in any classroom: the learner's attention.

---

## The Design Order (Fixed)

| Stage | Question | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Outcome | What will the learner be able to *do* after this that they cannot do now? | Observable outcome statements. |
| 2. Evidence | What would prove it? What would the learner produce, perform, or decide? | Assessment defined before any content exists. |
| 3. Experience | What sequence of activity produces that evidence? | The actual lessons, built last. |

The order is the doctrine. A lesson plan that arrives before an
outcome statement is returned to stage one, regardless of how good the
lesson is.

## The Laws

### 1. Outcomes Are Observable

"Understand," "appreciate," and "be familiar with" are not outcomes;
they are hopes, because no observer can see them happen. An outcome
uses a verb someone could watch or check: explain, build, diagnose,
compare, choose, perform, correct. The test of an outcome statement:
two independent observers could agree on whether a given learner has
met it.

### 2. The Alignment Audit

Three-way traceability, checked before anything ships:

- Every activity traces to an outcome.
- Every outcome has defined evidence.
- Every assessment measures a stated outcome, not an adjacent one.

Anything tracing to nothing is cut, however beloved. A fun activity
serving no outcome is entertainment wearing a lesson plan.

### 3. Cognitive Load Discipline

Working memory is the bottleneck of all instruction. The budget rules:

- One genuinely new concept at a time for novices. Two is a gamble;
  three is a guarantee that at least one did not land.
- Worked examples before independent production. Watching a solved
  problem is not cheating; it is how novices build the schema that
  experts forgot they needed.
- Strip extraneous load: decorative complexity, unexplained jargon,
  and split attention (text describing one thing while the visual
  shows another) all spend budget the concept needed.

### 4. Scaffold and Fade

Every support is temporary by design: the sentence starter, the
template, the hint sequence, the guided steps. A scaffold ships with
its fade plan, the named point where support withdraws and the learner
carries the load alone. A scaffold with no fade plan is not support;
it is a dependency being installed.

### 5. Misconception First

Learners are not blank; they arrive with a working model, and the
working model is usually wrong in a predictable way. Name the likely
misconception before designing the teaching, then design the moment
where the misconception visibly fails. Teaching the right model
without disturbing the wrong one produces a learner who holds both and
uses the old one under pressure.

### 6. Mastery Gates, Not Coverage

Progress is gated on demonstrated evidence, never on material
presented. "We covered chapter four" is a statement about the
teacher's calendar, not the learner's capability. Where a sequence
builds on itself, the gate is non-negotiable: advancing a learner past
an unmastered prerequisite purchases speed now with compounding
failure later.

### 7. Level Calibrates the Vehicle, Never the Respect

Designing for a five-year-old changes the pace, the vocabulary, the
session length, and the vehicle (story, play, repetition). It never
changes the underlying respect for the learner's capability. Content
dumbed down past its own integrity teaches the learner that the
subject is empty. Pace is not capability, at any age.

## Failure Modes

| Failure | Correction |
|---|---|
| **Coverage disease.** Success defined as "we got through the material." | Success is defined at stage two, as evidence. Material not serving evidence is cut. |
| **Activity theater.** Engaging activities with no traceable outcome. | The alignment audit. Engagement is fuel, not destination. |
| **Objective soup.** Twelve vague outcomes for a one-hour lesson. | Few, observable, honest. A one-hour lesson supports one to three real outcomes. |
| **Scaffold permanence.** Supports that never fade, producing dependent learners. | No scaffold without a fade plan, written at design time. |
| **Expert blind spot.** The designer can no longer see the steps novices need. | Worked examples and a novice walkthrough before shipping. The designer's fluency is evidence of nothing about the learner's path. |
| **Assessment mismatch.** Teaching one skill, testing an adjacent one. | Stage two binds the assessment to the outcome verb itself. |

## Compliance Check (Run Before Any Teaching Material Ships)

1. Was the outcome named before the activity existed?
2. Could two observers agree on whether a learner met each outcome?
3. Does every activity trace to an outcome and every outcome to
   evidence?
4. How many new concepts does each segment introduce, and is that
   number defensible for this audience?
5. Does every scaffold carry its fade plan?
6. Which misconception does this design surface and defeat?
7. Is progress gated on evidence or on coverage?

## Adversarial Evaluator Gate

**What would a hostile evaluator attack first?**

Rigidity. Backward design applied as bureaucracy produces outcome
statements written after the lesson to justify it, which is forward
design wearing paperwork. The defense is the audit direction: the
outcome must be able to *change* the activity. If no activity would
ever be cut or reshaped by re-reading the outcomes, the outcomes are
decoration and the doctrine has been laundered, not applied.

The second attack: mastery gates as gatekeeping, holding learners in
remediation loops that punish rather than teach. The gate is on
*prerequisites that compound*, not on perfection everywhere. Where
nothing downstream depends on a skill, the gate does not apply, and a
learner stalled at a gate is evidence about the design of the
instruction before it, not about the learner.
