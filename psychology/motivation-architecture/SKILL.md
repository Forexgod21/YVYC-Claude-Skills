# Motivation Architecture

## Doctrine Statement

Motivation is not a quantity to be increased. It is a structure with a
type, and the type predicts everything the quantity does not: whether
effort survives the absence of supervision, whether quality holds when
nobody is measuring, and whether people are still there in month six.

Two teams can hit the same output number, one running on interest and one
running on fear, and only one of those is a system. This skill audits
which one is being built.

## The Universal So-What

Most motivation interventions raise measurable output while degrading
motivation quality, and the degradation is invisible for one to two
quarters. By the time it surfaces as turnover, quiet compliance, or
collapsed discretionary effort, the intervention that caused it has
already been credited as a win and copied elsewhere. This skill catches
that trade before it ships.

## Theory as Mechanism

Self-Determination Theory is load-bearing here, not decorative. Its
specific claim is what makes the audit work: motivation quality is
governed by the satisfaction of three basic psychological needs, and
interventions that satisfy them move motivation toward internal
regulation while interventions that thwart them move it external, whatever
they do to short-term output.

## The Three Needs Audit

Every system gets scored on three axes. Score each as SATISFIED,
NEUTRAL, or THWARTED, with the evidence named.

| Need | The question | Thwarted looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Autonomy | Who chooses the methods, who hears the rationale, who has voice? | Directives without reasons, monitored process, no input channel |
| Competence | Can people see themselves getting better, with feedback they can act on? | Vague evaluation, unwinnable standards, feedback only when wrong |
| Relatedness | Do people matter to someone here beyond their output? | Purely transactional contact, isolation, interchangeable treatment |

Autonomy is checked first. It is the need most often thwarted by
well-intentioned design, because process visibility feels like management
and reads as surveillance.

## The Internalization Continuum

Motivation is located, not measured. Every system sits somewhere on this
line, and interventions move it.

| Position | Why the person acts | Durability |
|---|---|---|
| Amotivation | No reason; disengaged | None |
| External regulation | Reward or punishment outside them | Ends with the contingency |
| Introjected regulation | Guilt, shame, ego, avoiding looking bad | Costly, brittle, corrosive |
| Identified regulation | They accept the value of the goal | Strong |
| Integrated regulation | The goal fits who they are | Strongest |
| Intrinsic motivation | The activity itself is the reward | Self-sustaining |

The middle two positions are where most systems live and where most
systems break. Introjected regulation produces high output and looks like
commitment from the outside, which is why it survives review cycles that
should have caught it.

## The Architectural Rule

**Any intervention that moves regulation toward the external end of the
continuum is a loss disguised as a win, regardless of what it does to
output.**

This is the rule that gives the skill teeth. Output going up is not
evidence the intervention worked. Output going up while regulation moves
external is evidence the system now depends on the contingency remaining
in place forever.

## The Six-Step Audit Protocol

**Step 1: Locate current position.** Where on the continuum is the system
now? Name the evidence, not the impression.

**Step 2: Score the three needs.** SATISFIED, NEUTRAL, or THWARTED with
evidence per axis.

**Step 3: Identify the thwarting mechanism.** For each THWARTED need,
name the specific structural feature causing it. Not a person. A
structure.

**Step 4: Predict the intervention.** Before any change ships, state what
it does to each of the three needs and which direction it moves
regulation. This step is where most proposals fail and should.

**Step 5: Check the single-loop trap.** Distinguish the surface fix from
the underlying-belief fix. Adding a reward to a motivation problem is
almost always the single-loop patch: it changes behavior without
examining the belief structure producing the behavior. The internalization
question is the double-loop move. (Argyris and Schon, on single-loop and
double-loop learning.)

**Step 6: Name the measurement risk.** State what metric will improve
first if the intervention degrades motivation quality, so the false
positive is anticipated rather than celebrated.

## Common Interventions and What They Actually Do

| Intervention | Output effect | Regulation effect | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual performance bonus | Up near-term | Pushes external | Loss disguised as win |
| Public leaderboard | Up for the top decile | External for all, competence-starving below the top | Net negative at scale |
| Explaining the rationale for a directive | Neutral to up | Supports autonomy | Free gain, underused |
| Actionable specific feedback | Up sustained | Supports competence | Highest return available |
| Monitoring process rather than outcome | Up briefly | Thwarts autonomy hard | Reliable long-term damage |
| Choice of method within a fixed goal | Up sustained | Supports autonomy | Free gain, underused |

The two rows marked underused are the pattern: the interventions that
help most cost the least and get skipped because they do not look like
management action.

## The Leaderboard Case

Worth its own treatment because it is proposed constantly. A leaderboard
is a competence signal for the people at the top and a competence starver
for everyone else, and it moves regulation external for the entire
population including the winners. Under Step 4, it fails prediction. The
version that survives the audit measures each person against their own
prior performance rather than against each other.

## Adversarial Evaluator Gate

Before any motivation recommendation ships:

> If a hostile reviewer wanted to prove this intervention buys short-term
> compliance at the cost of long-term engagement, what evidence would they
> reach for first?

If that evidence exists and the recommendation does not address it, the
recommendation is not ready.

## The One Question This Skill Never Stops Asking

Does this intervention move people toward doing the work because they
value it, or toward doing it because something outside them is watching?
Everything else is detail.

## What This Skill Will Refuse

- Endorsing an intervention on output evidence alone
- Treating engagement metrics as motivation quality
- Recommending a contingency without stating what happens when it is
  removed
- Diagnosing a motivation problem as an individual failing when the
  structure is thwarting a need
- Presenting introjected regulation as commitment
