## What This Skill Does

Builds the load-bearing wall before the paint. Every piece gets one
spine statable in a sentence, tension that pulls the reader forward at
every moment through nested loops, a load-bearing order where every
unit earns its place, deliberate control of the reader's knowledge
state, proportion that matches emphasis to importance, and a spine
test run before any prose gets polished — because structure is the one
thing sentence craft cannot rescue.

## When It Activates

- Drafting or structurally editing stories, essays, articles, scripts
- Long-form content and book chapters
- Outlining
- Fixing pacing problems and middle-sag
- "This doesn't hold together" / "good parts that never become a
  whole"

## Installation

1. Create a folder named `narrative-spine-architecture` in your
   Claude skills location.
2. Place `SKILL.md` inside it.
3. Claude activates it automatically on structural writing work.
4. Pairs with `manuscript-revision-protocol` (its Pass 1 structural
   interrogation runs on this spine) and `prose-precision-protocol`
   (which polishes only after the spine holds).

## Example Invocations

> "Help me outline my essay."

Spine first: the one-sentence claim worth contesting, then the
load-bearing order that builds toward it, setups traced to payoffs,
and the reader's knowledge curve planned — structure that means
something, not a list of topics.

> "My story sags in the middle."

The tension diagnosis: almost always a central tension resolved too
early or a stretch of units that move nothing on the spine. The fix
is nested loops that keep the large question open and a scene audit
that cuts what does not advance the arc.

> "This piece has great lines but doesn't land."

The spine test: state the spine in one sentence — if it takes a
paragraph, the piece does not yet know what it is. Then walk every
unit for what it moves, trace setups and payoffs, and read the
skeleton alone for the gap the reader falls through.

> "Where should this scene go?"

The load-bearing-order question: sequence is an argument. Claude
places it where it moves the spine and where its setup precedes its
payoff — because reordering changes what the piece says, not just how
it flows.

## What You Get

- A spine you can state in one sentence — proof the piece knows itself
- Tension that renews instead of spending out early
- Every unit justified by what it moves
- Setups and payoffs that close every loop they open
- Proportion that tells the truth about what matters
- A skeleton tested before a single sentence gets polished

## What This Skill Will Refuse

- Structuring a piece with no statable spine
- Keeping a beautiful unit that moves nothing
- Payoffs without setups, setups without payoffs
- Proportion that lies about priority
- Polishing prose on an untested skeleton
