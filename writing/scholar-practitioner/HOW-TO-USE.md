## What This Skill Does

Scholar-Practitioner executes graduate-level academic writing at the
standard of a working professional who reads theory as a practitioner
and writes as a researcher. It operates in two modes — Execute for
submission-ready output, Teach for concept breakdown — and applies
three core quality gates to every deliverable: the adversarial evaluator
gate, theory-as-mechanism discipline, and applied-consequence testing.

## Program Calibration *(v1.1)*

The skill adapts to any graduate program. For best results, provide
four things up front:

1. **Program and institution** — calibrates depth, vocabulary, and
   evaluator expectations
2. **Citation style** — APA, Chicago, MLA, Turabian, AMA, or other;
   if unspecified, the skill defaults to your field's standard and
   confirms before output
3. **Rubric** — paste it or describe every criterion; the rubric
   governs all structural decisions
4. **Readings or sources** — the skill uses only sources you provide
   or verify; it never generates citations from memory

Provide none of them and the skill still executes at the highest
defensible standard — but it flags what's missing.

## Modes

### Execute (default)

Submission-ready output. No partial drafts. No placeholders.
Every deliverable ships complete with citations, proper formatting,
and full argument architecture.

Triggers: any request for a draft, post, paper, response, or review.

### Teach

Concept breakdown at full academic rigor. No dilution of depth —
just restructured for understanding before writing.

Triggers: "explain this," "I don't understand," "walk me through,"
"what does this mean," "break this down."

Returns to Execute automatically when the next request is a deliverable.

## The Three Quality Gates

Every deliverable runs through these before output:

**Adversarial Evaluator Gate**
What would a hostile evaluator attack first? That gets fixed before
submission readiness is declared.

**Theory-as-Mechanism Test**
Remove the citation. Does the argument collapse? If yes — integrated.
If no — the theory was decoration. Rebuild until it's load-bearing.
*(v1.1)* Theory selection is tested for fit, not fame: the theory that
actually fits the analytical problem beats the most prominent one every
time.

**Applied Consequence Test**
Does the analysis land somewhere real? An organizational outcome, a
leadership implication, a behavioral change. If it ends in abstraction,
it is not complete.

---

## Argument Architecture

Every piece follows this structure at every scale:

1. **Claim** — lead with the position
2. **Mechanism** — theory explains why
3. **Evidence** — citations support the mechanism
4. **Applied consequence** — real-world organizational implication
5. **Closing judgment** — disciplined analytical close, not a summary

## Deliverable Defaults

| Type | Default Length | Key Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Discussion post | 200–300 words | Open with example, cite readings, close with judgment |
| Response post | 100–150 words | Shorter than original, engage one idea, end with Socratic question |
| Paper section | Per rubric | Rubric-first, integrated theory throughout, full APA |

---

## What This Skill Will Not Do

- Produce partial, placeholder, or incomplete drafts
- Allow weak reasoning through without flagging it
- Use theory as decoration
- Invent citations or fabricate sources
- Soften evaluation to protect the writer's comfort
- Drift from the voice standard mid-session
