# Prose Precision Protocol

**Attribution:** YourVisionYourCreation LLC — yourvisionyourcreation.com
**Doctrine class:** YVYC original — writing category

---

## Universal So-What

At the line level, writing is decided by what survives the cut. Most
prose is not too plain — it is too crowded: hedges, throat-clearing,
abstractions, and redundant scaffolding burying the strong sentence
that was already there. Precision is subtraction before it is
addition. The protocol sharpens by removing what dilutes, then
strengthens what remains — in that order, because you cannot see the
strong sentence until the weak words around it are gone.

---

## Core Doctrine

### 1. Prerequisite — Structure Holds First

- Line editing runs only on sound structure: sentence surgery on a
  piece with no spine polishes what should be cut (confirm via
  narrative-spine-architecture before starting)
- If structural problems surface during line editing, the protocol
  stops and returns the piece to structural work — a beautifully
  edited paragraph that the structure will delete is waste

### 2. The Subtraction Pass — Cut First

The first pass removes, and it removes in known categories:

| Cut | Why |
|---|---|
| Hedges (somewhat, quite, rather, a bit, kind of) | They shrink claims the writer should either make or drop |
| Throat-clearing (it is important to note, needless to say, in order to) | Preamble that delays the sentence's actual work |
| Redundancy (each and every, end result, past history) | One word already carries the meaning |
| Filter words (I think, it seems, arguably) where the frame is understood | The reader knows whose view this is |
| Intensifiers propping weak words (very unique, really huge) | A precise word needs no crutch |

The rule: if the sentence survives the cut with meaning intact, the
cut word was dilution.

### 3. Verbs Carry, Nouns Anchor

- The verb is the sentence's engine: strong, specific, active verbs
  do the work that strings of adverbs and adjectives fake — "sprinted"
  beats "ran very quickly"
- Nominalizations are un-buried: "made a decision" becomes "decided,"
  "gave consideration to" becomes "considered" — the buried verb is
  freed and the sentence shortens and quickens
- Concrete nouns beat abstract ones where the point is to be seen: the
  reader pictures "a rusted hinge," not "a state of disrepair" —
  abstraction is used deliberately, never by default

### 4. Rhythm and Sentence Variety

- Sentence length is music: all-long lulls, all-short hammers,
  variety carries — the pattern is heard by reading aloud, the only
  reliable instrument
- The strong position is the sentence's end: the last word rings
  longest, so the sentence lands on its most important word, not on a
  trailing qualifier
- Paragraph shape matters too: a paragraph is a unit of thought, and
  a page of uniform blocks is as tiring as uniform sentences — white
  space is pacing

### 5. Precision of Meaning

- The nearly-right word is a defect: "reticent" is not "reluctant,"
  "comprise" is not "compose" — the word that means exactly the thing
  beats the word that gestures at it
- Concrete beats vague on every axis the point requires: a number
  beats "many," a name beats "a thing," a specific beats a category —
  except where deliberate generality serves
- Clichés are dead precision: they were vivid once and are now
  invisible — replaced with the fresh phrasing the cliché is standing
  in for, or cut

### 6. The Precision Passes and Their Limit

- Read-aloud is mandatory: the ear catches what the eye forgives —
  clunks, repetitions, breath failures, and rhythm ruts surface only
  by mouth
- Echo hunt: unintended repeated words and pet constructions, found
  and varied
- The overcorrection guard: precision serves voice, it does not
  sand voice away — a piece edited until every sentence is uniformly
  tight can lose the rhythm, warmth, and idiosyncrasy that made it
  worth reading. The last question is always: does it still sound
  like a person? Voice outranks polish.

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure | Cause | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Editing a piece that should be restructured | Line work on a broken spine | Structure holds first — prerequisite gate |
| Prose reads crowded and slow | Dilution never cut | The subtraction pass, by category |
| Sentences feel weak despite effort | Adjective-adverb stacks faking strength | Verbs carry; un-bury nominalizations |
| Flat, monotonous rhythm | No length variety | Read aloud; vary sentence length; land on the strong word |
| "Almost right" words muddy the meaning | Gesturing vocabulary | Precision of meaning; the exact word |
| Edited until it sounds like nobody | Overcorrection past voice | The voice guard — does it still sound like a person? |

---

## Non-Negotiables

1. Structure holds before line editing begins.
2. Subtraction comes before addition — cut the dilution first.
3. Verbs carry the sentence; buried verbs get freed.
4. The sentence lands on its most important word.
5. The nearly-right word is replaced with the right one.
6. Voice outranks polish — precision never sands away the person.
