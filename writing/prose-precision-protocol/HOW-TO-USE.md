## What This Skill Does

Sharpens prose by subtraction first, addition second. Once structure
is confirmed sound, Claude runs a subtraction pass that cuts dilution
by category, frees buried verbs, lands sentences on their strongest
word, replaces the nearly-right word with the right one, and tunes
rhythm by ear — with a voice guard that stops precision from sanding
the writer away, because a piece edited until it sounds like nobody
has been over-edited.

## When It Activates

- Line editing any prose whose structure is already sound
- Cutting bloat, tightening, sharpening
- Fixing flat, muddy, or crowded writing
- "Make this tighter" / "strengthen these sentences"

## Installation

1. Create a folder named `prose-precision-protocol` in your Claude
   skills location.
2. Place `SKILL.md` inside it.
3. Claude activates it automatically on line-level editing.
4. Runs AFTER `narrative-spine-architecture` (structure first) and
   shares its discipline with `manuscript-revision-protocol`'s Pass 3.

## Example Invocations

> "Tighten this paragraph."

The subtraction pass first: hedges, throat-clearing, redundancy, and
propping intensifiers cut by category — then the strong sentence that
was buried gets its verbs freed and its landing word moved to the
end, with a read-aloud rhythm check.

> "My writing feels weak but I can't say why."

Usually adjective-adverb stacks faking the work a verb should do.
Claude un-buries the nominalizations, swaps the gesturing words for
exact ones, and varies the sentence lengths the flatness is hiding in.

> "Is this too plain?"

Often the opposite — too crowded. The diagnosis runs the subtraction
categories first, because most "plain" prose is a strong sentence
buried under dilution, not a sentence that needs more words.

> "Edit this but don't kill my voice."

The voice guard is built in: precision serves voice, never sands it
away. The last question on every pass is whether it still sounds like
a person — and voice outranks polish when they conflict.

## What You Get

- Prose sharpened by removing what dilutes, then strengthening what
  remains
- Verbs doing the work instead of adverb stacks faking it
- Sentences that land on the word that matters
- Exact words in place of the nearly-right ones
- Rhythm tuned by ear
- A voice that survives the edit intact

## What This Skill Will Refuse

- Line editing a structurally broken piece
- Adding words before cutting dilution
- Leaving verbs buried in nominalizations
- Accepting the nearly-right word
- Editing past the voice into uniform, personless polish
